## Probe 1. Chain use

Exact target, in one sentence. For a task built so that the answer needs k written steps, the target is acc(f) minus acc(1), where acc(f) is the accuracy against the built ground truth of the answer the model gives when only the first fraction f of its own written chain is kept (acc(1) is the full chain).

| name | what it computes | source | assumption under which it tracks the target | how it misleads when the assumption fails | cost for a small model |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Early answering, area over the curve | For each chain cut after 0, 1, ..., n sentences, the share of cuts whose answer equals the answer given with the full chain. AOC is a weighted sum over chain lengths, weight the share of samples of that length. No formula is given in the paper. | Lanham et al. 2023, Measuring Faithfulness in Chain-of-Thought Reasoning, arXiv 2307.13702, section 2.3 (opened here, HTML) | The full-chain answer is correct, so agreement with it equals accuracy. A flat curve is read as post-hoc reasoning. | Scores agreement, not accuracy. If the full chain is wrong, a cut that agrees counts as no loss. The paper says post-hoc and unfaithful are not equivalent and that AOC varies only weakly with the accuracy gain from the chain. | n + 1 forced answers per chain, each one forward pass of the prompt plus the cut chain plus a short answer. About n + 1 times the cost of one prompt. |
| Forced-answer accuracy by truncation point | Cut the trace at an intermediate step, append the end-of-thinking token and a fixed answer prompt, read the softmax over the four option letters (11 output tokens, temperature 0). The paper plots agreement with the final answer by position and also correctness. | Boppana et al. 2026, Reasoning Theater, arXiv 2603.05488, sections 3.3 and 5, appendices A.2, F, G (opened here, first 100000 of 105206 characters) | The injected answer prompt gives the answer the model would reach by continuing. The cut leaves the text on the model's own distribution. | Off-policy cut: on the 1.5B distill, forced answering is below the probe. On 14B and 32B it drops in the second half of the trace, which the authors read as a plausible answer in place of the belief. Both are authors' readings, not tested here. On GPQA-D both methods are wrong about 40 percent of the time early. | One forward pass per truncation point, up to 11 new tokens. Same order as early answering. |
| Attention probe on the hidden state at the cut | A trained attention probe reads activations at the cut and predicts the final answer. Probe accuracy against position is compared with forced answering and with a monitor (Table 1, performativity rate as slope difference of quadratic fits per 5 percent bin). | Boppana et al. 2026, arXiv 2603.05488, sections 3, 5, 7 (opened here, same fetch) | The final answer is linearly or attentively decodable from the cut state, and the probe was trained on traces of the same task family. | The probe predicts the model's final answer, not the truth. A probe trained on one task may read task statistics. The paper reports results are sensitive to window and threshold (reading.md row). | Probe training on cached activations plus one pass per trace. No extra generation. Needs a labelled set of traces. |
| Resampling importance of a step | For sentence S_i, KL of the answer distribution after removing S_i and regenerating from that point against the distribution with S_i kept, about 100 rollouts per position. The counterfactual variant keeps only resamples that differ from S_i (cosine below 0.8). | Bogdan, Macar, Nanda, Conmy 2025, Thought Anchors, arXiv 2506.19143, sections 2.2, 2.3, 3.2 (opened here, first 100000 of 124053 characters) | Regenerating from the cut stands in for the distribution over continuations. The answer distribution is estimated from enough rollouts. | Measures the effect of one step, not the effect of cutting the tail. Scores are on 20 MATH problems, one 14B model. Overdetermined steps score low. Attention-based variants carry position confounds (their appendix C.4). | About 100 rollouts per step. For a chain of n steps, 100 n generations. Dominant cost of the four. |
| Outcome distribution per prefix on a base path | Distribution of the final answer given each prefix o_t of a greedy path, estimated by sampling, with change point and kernel pooling to cut samples. | Bigelow et al. 2026, Forking Fast, arXiv 2608.19611 (opened earlier, reading.md, TP builder; definition not re-read here) | Samples from a prefix are independent and the greedy path is typical. | Per reading.md: the pooling advantage is lost at large forks, and intervals cover 48 to 64 percent against 90 nominal. | S samples per prefix, S = 30 to 200 in the paper. |

Note on the target. Only the second and fourth rows can be scored against ground truth as written. The first scores agreement with the full chain.

## Probe 2. Order of difficulty

Exact target, in one sentence. For a fixed item set X, the target is the rank correlation (Kendall tau) between the vectors d_base(x) = 1 minus P(model gives the correct answer to x) for the base model and d_post(x) for the post-trained model, where the probability is over the model's sampling; the probe asks whether 1 minus tau exceeds what sampling noise alone gives.

| name | what it computes | source | assumption under which it tracks the target | how it misleads when the assumption fails | cost for a small model |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Per-item pass rate over G rollouts | p-hat(x) = (1/G) sum over rollouts of binary correctness; the paper estimates p(x) = E[r_acc(x, y)] (Eq. 11) with G = 16 and keeps prompts with p-hat between 0.2 and 0.8 (balanced filtering). | Bae, Hong et al. 2025, Online Difficulty Filtering, arXiv 2504.03380, sections 3.1 to 3.4, 4.1, 4.2 and Limitations (opened here, first 100000 of 138046 characters) | The correctness reward is a faithful label, and G is large enough that p-hat ranks items. | With G = 16, p-hat has a binomial step of 1/16 and many ties at 0 and 1, so tau between two models is biased toward 1 or undefined for the saturated items. Validated on math only. | G generations per item per model. 16 times the items for one pass. |
| 2PL item difficulty b_j | The 2PL model p = logistic(a_j (theta - b_j)) (Eq. 2); item parameters fitted by MCMC on 102 models of the Open LLM Leaderboard and then held fixed; ability theta by MAP. | Hofmann et al. 2025, Fluid Language Model Benchmarking, arXiv 2509.11106, sections 3 and 4.1 (opened earlier, reading.md, TG builder; not re-read here) | One latent ability explains the models, so one item order holds for all of them, and items keep their parameters across checkpoints. | If post-training reorders items, the model with fixed b_j cannot show it. Refitting b_j per model needs enough models or enough responses per item; the 102-model fit does not give a per-model order. This is the assumption the probe tests. | Fitting needs many models. For one small model, b_j is not identified from one response vector. |
| Prediction depth | The earliest layer l from which every k-NN probe (k = 30) on training embeddings agrees with the final classification; depth 0 if all probes agree. | Baldock, Maennel, Neyshabur 2021, Deep Learning Through the Lens of Example Difficulty, arXiv 2106.09647, section 2.1, Figures 4 and 6 (opened here, first 100000 of 147109 characters) | The layer at which a prediction settles orders examples by difficulty, and the order is stable across seeds. The paper reports correlation with output margin about minus 0.70 on CIFAR10 with VGG16. | Defined for classification on four image sets and three small networks. The linear bounds on consensus-consistency are empirical. The consensus estimate is biased because one ensemble supplies the class and the score (their A.6.3). Not shown for language models or free-form answers. | One forward pass per item with k-NN probes at each layer against the training embeddings. Memory grows with training set size times layers. |
| Forgetting count | Number of times example i goes from correct at step t to incorrect at step t+1 during training; never learned counts as infinite; unforgettable if learned and never lost. | Toneva et al. 2018, An Empirical Study of Example Forgetting during Deep Neural Network Learning, arXiv 1812.05159, section 3, 3.1, 4, 6 (opened here, ar5iv HTML) | Forgetting counts rank examples the same way across seeds and, loosely, across architectures. Reported mean Pearson correlation of counts across 10 seeds is 89.2 percent. | A lower bound, since counted only when the example is in a minibatch. Ranking stabilizes only after about 75 epochs. Needs the training run, so it cannot compare two finished checkpoints that were not logged. Image sets only. | Per-example correctness logged at each step of training. Free during training; no extra pass. |

Note on the target. The first row is the only one that computes d_base(x) and d_post(x) directly. The other three order items by a property of a training run or of the representation, and their agreement with d(x) is a separate claim that no source above tests for post-training of a language model.
