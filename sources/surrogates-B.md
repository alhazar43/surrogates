# Surrogates for two probes (SweepB)

Rung of every source below is "source, full text" unless marked. Definitions come from the section named. Nothing here says which surrogate suits this project.

## Probe 1. Cue or rule: when cue and rule agree in training, the cheaper one wins

Exact target. For a model trained on data where a cue feature s and a rule feature t both predict the label, the target is the probability that the trained model uses s rather than t on inputs where they disagree, as a function of the cost of computing s and of t.

Rows 1.1, 1.2 and 1.4 stand in for "which one is used". Rows 1.3 and 1.5 stand in for "cheaper".

## Probe 2. Gap: a longer gap between repeats of a rare task hurts narrow models more

Exact target. For a model of width N trained on a stream where a rare task recurs after g steps of other tasks, the target is the interaction between g and N in the loss on that task after the recurrence.

No source found measures that interaction. Each row below measures one margin of it, forgetting against steps since last use, or rare-fact accuracy against model size. The claim is a product of two margins and no row tests it.

| name | what it computes | source | assumption under which it tracks the target | how it misleads when the assumption fails | cost for a small model |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 Forgetting at k steps after last use | A training algorithm (A, alpha, k)-forgets example z if attack A succeeds on z at rate at most alpha, k steps after z was last used (Definition 3.1). The attack is membership inference (images) or canary exposure(s, f) = log2 abs(S) - log2 Rank(s, S, loss) (speech, language) | Jagielski, Thakkar, Tramer, Ippolito, Lee, Carlini, Zhang 2023, Measuring Forgetting of Memorized Training Examples, ICLR 2023, arXiv 2207.00099, Sections 2.2, 3.1, 3.2, 4.2, 4.3, Appendix C.3 | Attack success rises with how much the model stores of the example, and reference models calibrate the loss | The authors call it heuristic and say it cannot prove forgetting happened (Section 6). Per gap and width, one pair of runs |
