        $\rightarrow$ Select high-information items $I_j(\hat{\theta}) = a_j^2 \hat{p}_j(1-\hat{p}_j)$ to estimate score from $n \ll N$ items.

        Replace $G=5$ group rollouts with a single rollout ($G=1$) using IRT expectation $\hat{p}_j(\theta_t) = \sigma(a_j(\theta_t - b_j))$:\\[1mm]
        {\centering $\hat{A}_j = 2\,a_j^\gamma\bigl(r_j - \hat{p}_j(\theta_t)\bigr), \qquad \gamma \in \{0, 1\}$\par}
      }
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Terminology Bridge: LLM vs.\ Psychometric IRT}
  \vspace{1mm}
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.48}
  \setlength{\tabcolsep}{7pt}
  \scriptsize
  \begin{tabular}{>{\bfseries\color{NavyHeader}}l >{\color{BlueSFT}\bfseries}l >{\color{PurpleIRT}\bfseries}l l}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Concept} & \textbf{LLM / ML Term} & \textbf{Psychometric / IRT Term} & \textbf{Notation} \\
    \midrule
    1. Test-Taker &
    Model checkpoint $\pi_\phi$ ($K=16$ draws) &
    Respondent with ability $\theta$ &
    $\pi_\phi \;\leftrightarrow\; \theta$ \\
    \rowcolor{LightBg}
    2. Item Easiness &
    Base initial pass rate ($p_{\mathrm{base},j}$) &
    Within-model item intercept ($\mu_{0j}$) &
    $\mu_{0j} = \mathrm{logit}(p_{\mathrm{base},j})$ \\
    3. Sharpening &
    Inverse temperature ($1/T$) or RL &
    Response curve slope ($\tau$) &
    $\tau \;\leftrightarrow\; 1/T$ \\
    \rowcolor{LightBg}
    4. Chance Floor &
    4-choice guessing rate ($25\%$) &
    3PL guessing parameter ($c = 1/K$) &
    $c = 0.25$ \\
    5. Learning Signal &
    \texttt{GRPO} reward variance ($\hat{\sigma}_j^2 > 0$) &
    1PL item Fisher information &
    $I_j = p_j(1 - p_j)$ \\
    \rowcolor{LightBg}
    6. Prompt Bias &
    Option-order gap (\texttt{A/B/C/D} swap) &
    Differential Item Functioning (DIF) &
    $D = |p_{\mathrm{ord0}} - p_{\mathrm{ord1}}|$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}

  \vspace{3.5mm}
  \card{NavyHeader}{
    \textbf{\color{NavyHeader}Key Distinction:} \textbf{Population IRT} calibrates $(a_j, b_j)$ across 102 \emph{different} models (between-model). \textbf{Within-model IRT} tracks how a \emph{single} model's pass probability $p_j$ evolves across repeated rollouts during training.
  }
\end{frame}

% SLIDE 9: MECHANISM — CURVE STEEPENING VS. SUPPORT SHIFT
% ==============================================================================
\begin{frame}{Mechanism: Curve Steepening vs.\ Support Shift}
  \begin{columns}[T, onlytextwidth]
    \begin{column}{0.56\textwidth}
      \vspace{-1mm}
      \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/talk_Fig05_prf_slope_and_distractor_pivot.png}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.42\textwidth}
      \vspace{1mm}
      \card{PurpleIRT}{
        \textbf{\color{PurpleIRT}1. Within-Model Response Curve}\\
        Post-training pass rate as a function of initial log-odds $\mu_{0j}$:\\[1mm]
        {\centering $p_j = \sigma\!\bigl(\alpha + \tau(\mu_{0j} - \mu_c)\bigr), \quad \mu_c = \mathrm{logit}(0.25)$\par}\vspace{1mm}
        $\bullet$ \textbf{Slope $\tau \leftrightarrow 1/T$}: Sharpens existing preferences.\\
        $\bullet$ \textbf{Shift $\alpha$}: Adds new knowledge support.
      }
      \vspace{2mm}
      \card{RedGRPO}{
        \textbf{\color{RedGRPO}2. Why $c = 25\%$ Is the Polarity Pivot}\\
        $\bullet$ \texttt{GRPO} steepens the slope ($\mathbf{\tau = 1.85 > 1}$):\\
        \quad -- When $p_{\mathrm{base},j} > 25\%$, the modal answer is correct $\rightarrow$ sharpening increases accuracy.\\
        \quad -- When $p_{\mathrm{base},j} < 25\%$, a \textbf{wrong distractor is modal} $\rightarrow$ sharpening locks in the wrong answer ($p_j \to 0$).\\
        $\bullet$ \texttt{CoT-SFT} ($\mathbf{\tau = 0.42 < 1}$) shifts support upward on hard items.
      }
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

        $\bullet$ $\mathrm{corr}(J_j u_\perp, a_j^{\mathrm{pop}}) = \mathbf{0.072}$.\\

      }
