## Part 2: Slide-by-Slide Detailed Plan (12 Slides, ~30–40 Min Talk)

### Act I: The Hook & What Static Cross-Model Item Parameters $(a_j^{\text{pop}}, b_j^{\text{pop}})$ CANNOT Do (Slides 1–3)

- **Slide 1 — Title & Framing the Psychometric Question**
  - **Title**: *Evaluating and Training Large Language Models: What Can (and Cannot) Item Parameters Tell Us?*
  - **Key Message**: Recent ML benchmarks (*Fluid Benchmarking*, *tinyBenchmarks*, *SEELE*, *RIDE*) calibrate 2PL item parameters $(a_j^{\text{pop}}, b_j^{\text{pop}})$ across a crowd of 100+ LLMs and use them for short-test adaptive evaluation (CAT / $\hat{\theta}$), item-residual diagnostics, and RL advantage weighting. We audit this paradigm across `1.488M` evaluation rollouts (`E1`: 4,918 students vs. 61 LLMs on 704 Eedi items; `W0`: 102 LLMs on MMLU; `S1`: 7 Base/SFT/GRPO states $\times$ 48,000 rollouts; `Q1`: 24 RL checkpoints/finals $\times$ 48,000 rollouts; and `Unit M1`).
  - **Visual**: 2×2 preview matrix contrasting **Cross-Respondent Static $(a_j^{\text{pop}}, b_j^{\text{pop}})$** against **Within-Model Item Statistics $(L_{\text{base},j}, p_{s,j})$** across **Evaluation** and **Training**.

- **Slide 2 — `CANNOT #1`: Static Cross-Respondent 2PL Parameters Do Not Transfer Across Populations or Isolate Trajectory Slopes**
  - **Key Message**:
    1. **Human Students vs. LLMs (`Unit E1`, `F1 Panel A`)**: On **`J = 704` Eedi diagnostic math items** (`results/E1/difficulty.csv`, `notes/units/E1.md:84`), human 2PL item difficulty $b_j^{\text{human}}$ (`4,918` students) has almost zero rank agreement with 61-LLM item difficulty $b_j^{\text{LLM}}$ (**Spearman $\rho = +0.089$**; raw Pearson $r = -0.013$, OLS slope $-0.76$). Moreover, **`61/61` LLMs fall below $Z_h = -1.96$** under the human 2PL response model (`E1/llm_scores.csv`, `zh_2pl`, `closure.md:25`). Per `closure.md:29`, the 61-LLM $b_j^{\text{LLM}}$ values rest on the rejected 61-respondent calibration and span `[-1574, +912]`, so a few extreme items dominate the raw Pearson and only the rank correlation ($\rho = +0.089$) is meaningful.
    2. **102-LLM Discrimination $a_j^{\text{pop}}$ vs. Within-Model Post-Training Shift (`T9r` & `W0`, `F1 Panel B`)**: Across **`J = 1,472` MMLU items**, 102-LLM discrimination $a_j^{\text{pop}}$ is **not** orthogonal to a single 1B model's 3-seed mean token-0 post-training shift $\Delta L_j$: Pearson $r = +0.392$ ($R^2 = 15.4\%$, OLS slope $+1.43$) for `GRPO` step 666 and $r = -0.470$ ($R^2 = 22.1\%$, OLS slope $-0.86$) for `SFT` step 70. Why? Because $a_j^{\text{pop}}$ already correlates **$r = +0.568$** with the 1B model's own initial item log-odds $L_{\text{base},j}$.
  - **Visual**: **Figure F1** (`F1_non_ergodicity.png`).

- **Slide 3 — `CANNOT #2`: Why Static $(a_j^{\text{pop}}, b_j^{\text{pop}})$ Fails to Isolate Item Learning (`Q2-A`) — Within-Model Prior Scaling**
  - **Key Message**: In `Q2-A` (`report-q1q2.md:43-44`), using 102-LLM item parameters to extract IRT item residuals $c_{s,j}$ tied classical raw change (`0/6` in `S1`, `1/12` in `Q1`). Why? Because post-training item shifts $\Delta L_{s,j} = L_{s,j} - L_{\text{base},j}$ are strongly coupled to the model's **own** initial item log-odds $L_{\text{base},j}$:
    $$\hat{\tau}_s = r(L_s, L_{\text{base}}) \cdot \frac{\sigma(L_s)}{\sigma(L_{\text{base}})}, \qquad \text{OLS slope}(\Delta L_s \text{ on } L_{\text{base}}) = \hat{\tau}_s - 1$$
    - **Token-0 Forced Reads (`T9r/items_wide.csv`, clip $\varepsilon = 10^{-4}$, `F2 Panel A`)**:
      - **SFT (`t9_sft`)**: Seed 0 has OLS slope $-0.456$ ($\hat{\tau}_0 = 0.544, R^2 = 59.2\%$; across 3 seeds, $R^2 = 59.2\%\text{–}87.3\%$, $\hat{\tau} \in [0.354, 0.544]$, mean `0.430`, with $r = 0.806\text{–}0.835$, mean `0.822`, and $\sigma_s/\sigma_{\text{base}} = 0.440\text{–}0.652$, mean `0.523`).
      - **GRPO (`t9_rl`)**: Seed 0 has OLS slope $+0.868$ ($\hat{\tau}_0 = 1.868, R^2 = 48.7\%$; across 3 seeds at $\varepsilon = 10^{-4}$, $R^2 = 48.7\%\text{–}51.4\%$, $\hat{\tau} \in [1.868, 1.920]$, mean `1.890`, with $r = 0.900\text{–}0.910$, mean `0.904`, and $\sigma_s/\sigma_{\text{base}} = 2.07\text{–}2.13$, mean `2.091`). Note that the upper diagonal band of GRPO points in `F2 Panel A` comes from items clipped at $p = 1 - 10^{-4}$ (at $\varepsilon = 10^{-6}$, GRPO $R^2$ is $63.5\%\text{–}68.5\%$), and `T9r/summary.md` reports a different $\tau$ definition (`SFT 0.342–0.585`, `RL 2.089–2.170`).
    - **$K=32$ Sampled Rollouts (`Q2g/s1/residuals.csv`, `p_tilde`, `F2 Panel B`)**:
      - On the $K=32$ draws used in `Q2-A`, `F3`, and `F4`, $R^2(\Delta L, L_{\text{base}})$ is **`27%–29%` (`0.27–0.29`) for SFT** and **`9.5%–10.1%` (`0.095–0.101`) for GRPO**.
      - **SFT ($K=32$)**: per-seed $r(L_s, L_{\text{base}}) = 0.592\text{–}0.611$ (mean `0.600`, pooled counts `0.629`), $\sigma_s/\sigma_{\text{base}} = 0.82\text{–}0.83$ (mean `0.823`), $\hat{\tau} = 0.49\text{–}0.50$ (mean `0.494`).
      - **GRPO ($K=32$)**: per-seed $r(L_s, L_{\text{base}}) = 0.731\text{–}0.746$ (mean `0.739`, pooled counts `0.765`), $\sigma_s/\sigma_{\text{base}} = 1.91\text{–}1.99$ (mean `1.946`, pooled `2.21`), $\hat{\tau} = 1.43\text{–}1.46$ (mean `1.438`).
    - **Free-Text CoT Answer Tokens (`T9g/s3/matched.csv`, clip $\varepsilon = 10^{-4}$, descriptive computation)**:
      - SFT reorders item log-odds ($r = 0.275\text{–}0.301$, mean `0.289`) while increasing dispersion ($\sigma_s = 7.20\text{–}7.66$ vs. $\sigma_b = 3.15\text{–}3.18$, ratio $2.27\text{–}2.41$, mean `2.35`), giving $\hat{\tau} = 0.62\text{–}0.73$ (mean `0.68`).
  - **Visual**: **Figure F2** (`F2_prior_scaling.png`) + **Table T1**.

### Act II: `A helps B` — What Subject-Level $(a_m^{\text{pop}}, b_m^{\text{pop}})$ Tell Us & The Base-Log-Odds Confound Check (Slides 4–6)

- **Slide 4 — `CAN #1` (Part A): The 15 MMLU Subjects in the $(\bar{a}_m^{\text{pop}}, \bar{b}_m^{\text{pop}})$ Plane (`W0/items_bank.csv`)**
  - **Key Message**: Aggregating `W0/items_bank.csv` across the $n = 15$ MMLU subjects shows a strong negative relationship between subject mean 102-LLM discrimination $\bar{a}_m^{\text{pop}}$ and subject mean difficulty $\bar{b}_m^{\text{pop}}$ (**$r = -0.8841, R^2 = 78.2\%$**, OLS slope $-2.01$, intercept $+2.04$):
    - **High-$\bar{a}_m^{\text{pop}}$ (`0.69–1.45`), Low-$\bar{b}_m^{\text{pop}}$ (`-0.99 to +0.72`) Subjects** (*Marketing, Sociology, Prehistory, HS Geography, World History, College Biology, Philosophy, Clinical Knowledge*): Factual/declarative subjects where accuracy scales steeply with model size across the 102-LLM leaderboard.
    - **Low-$\bar{a}_m^{\text{pop}}$ (`0.28–0.55`), High-$\bar{b}_m^{\text{pop}}$ (`+0.25 to +2.30`) Subjects** (*HS Math, Conceptual Physics, HS Statistics, Elementary Math, Econometrics, HS Macroeconomics*): Quantitative/calculation-heavy subjects where standard base models have flatter discrimination and higher difficulty (*HS Microeconomics* sits between the two groups at $\bar{a}_m = 0.61, \bar{b}_m = +0.58$).
  - **Visual**: **Figure F3 (Panel A)** (`F3_double_dissociation_15domains.png`).

- **Slide 5 — `CAN #1` (Part B): Subject-Level SFT vs. GRPO Gains ($R^2 = 76.9\%$) & The Base-Log-Odds Confound Check**
  - **Key Message**:
    1. **Observed Subject-Level Pattern on Seen `S1` States (`F3 Panel B`)**: Across the $n = 15$ subject means of the seen `S1` states (`Q2g/s1/residuals.csv`), the operator difference $\overline{\Delta L}_{\text{SFT},m} - \overline{\Delta L}_{\text{GRPO},m}$ correlates **$r = +0.8771$ ($R^2 = 76.9\%, p = 1.8\times 10^{-5}$, OLS slope $+0.68$, intercept $-0.48$) with $\bar{b}_m^{\text{pop}}$** and **$r = -0.8015$ ($R^2 = 64.2\%, p = 0.0003$) with $\bar{a}_m^{\text{pop}}$** (while GRPO gain alone correlates $r = +0.7464, p = 0.0014$ with $\bar{a}_m^{\text{pop}}$; on the unseen `Q1` arms, $r(\bar{a}_m^{\text{pop}}, \overline{\Delta L}_m) = 0.64\text{–}0.72$ descriptively).
    2. **Post-Hoc Group Counts (`±0.1` Logit Threshold)**: At a post-hoc $\pm 0.1$ logit threshold, SFT leads on **`5/6` low-$\bar{a}_m$ (`0.28–0.55`) subjects** (*Elementary Math* `+1.491` logits over GRPO, *HS Math* `+0.772`; SFT itself gains `+1.20 to +1.65` logits on math while GRPO changes by `-0.575` on *Econometrics*, `-0.192` on *Macroeconomics*, and `-0.035` on *Physics*; *HS Statistics* `-0.077` is a tie), whereas GRPO leads on **`8/8` high-$\bar{a}_m$ (`0.69–1.45`) subjects** (*HS Microeconomics* `-0.084`, $\bar{a}_m = 0.61$ is in neither group).
    3. **Critical Psychometric Confound Check (`review-oct26-slide-plan.md:66`)**: Why does subject $\bar{b}_m^{\text{pop}}$ predict $\overline{\Delta L}_{\text{SFT},m} - \overline{\Delta L}_{\text{GRPO},m}$ so strongly across the 15 subject means?   - **Key Message**: In `Unit Q1`, we trained 12 RL runs (4 arms $\times$ 3 seeds, 333 updates) from `t9_base` comparing `grpo` ($G=5$) against three $G=1$ baseline-subtracted critics (`ctt` pass-rate band, unweighted 2PL `pop0`, and $a_j^{\text{pop}}$-weighted 2PL `pop1`):

In Seed 0, those 4 runs reach **`83.7%` (`grpo`), `91.5%` (`ctt`), `93.9%` (`pop0`), and `94.5%` (`pop1`)** of their final gain within the first **83 updates** (`0.382 -> 0.455–0.474` at u83).

- **Slide 12 — Synthesis: What Item Parameters CAN (and CANNOT) Tell Us**
  - **Key Message**:
    - **What Cross-Model Static $(a_j^{\text{pop}}, b_j^{\text{pop}})$ CANNOT Do**:
      1. **Transfer Across Respondent Regimes (`E1`, `F1`)**: Human 2PL $b_j^{\text{human}}$ is rank-orthogonal to 61-LLM $b_j^{\text{LLM}}$ on 704 Eedi items ($\rho = +0.089$), and `61/61` LLMs have $Z_h < -1.96$.
      2. **Isolate Item-Level Learning Without Accounting for $L_{\text{base},j}$ (`Q2-A`, `F1 B`, `F2`)**: 102-LLM $a_j^{\text{pop}}$ correlates $r = +0.568$ with $L_{\text{base},j}$ (and $r = +0.392$ with GRPO $\Delta L_j$, $-0.470$ with SFT $\Delta L_j$), so IRT item residuals tie classical raw change (`0/6` in `S1`, `1/12` in `Q1`).
      3. **Beat Unbiased Random Sampling at $n=100$ (`Q2a/Q2c`, `F6 B`)**: High-$a_j^{\text{pop}}$ CAT over-selects Sociology (`40%–49%`), losing to `classical` random sampling by $n=100$ in `Q2c` (`0.063 vs. 0.050`) and in `19/21` cells of `Q2a` ($k=16$).
      4. **Outperform Unweighted RL from Base (`Q1`, `F7`)**: All 12 `Q1` runs across `grpo, ctt, pop0, pop1` converge to `0.4685–0.4748` (`0.63 pp` band).
    - **What Item Parameters & Multi-Draw Psychometrics CAN Tell Us (With Exact Scope)**:
      1. **Subject-Level Construct Map & Confound Check (`F3`, $n = 15$ MMLU subjects)**: $(\bar{a}_m^{\text{pop}}, \bar{b}_m^{\text{pop}})$ separates declarative recall from quantitative subjects ($r = -0.8841$) and explains $R^2 = 76.9\%$ ($r = +0.8771$) of $\overline{\Delta L}_{\text{SFT},m} - \overline{\Delta L}_{\text{GRPO},m}$ on seen `S1` states—while our confound check shows that $\bar{b}_m^{\text{pop}}$ tracks subject Base log-odds $\overline{L}_{\text{base},m}$ ($r = -0.94$; partial $r = 0.17\text{–}0.25$; item-level $R^2 = 17.7\%$).
      2. **Explaining Metric Disagreements (`F4`, `F5`, `F6 A`)**: Shows why `Mean@16` (`GRPO 0.479 > SFT 0.439`) and `Maj@16` (`SFT 0.504/0.555* > GRPO 0.481/0.490*`, `2/3` seeds) cross over, why seen `S1` stage attribution flips from `59.5/40.5` (Accuracy) to `20.9/79.1` ($\hat{\theta}$, via `A3_spearman_c_a`, narrowed to `3/12` on unseen `Q1`), and why single-draw $l_z^{(1)} = +2.24$ differs from 32-trial $l_z^{(32)} = -482.3$ on `S1` GRPO.
      3. **Within-Model Item Parameters (`F2`, `F8`, `Unit M1`)**: The model's **own** item parameters ($L_{\text{base},j}$, $\hat{\tau}_s$, and $p_{\text{SFT},j} \in [0.2, 0.8]$) quantify operator scaling across modalities and motivate the pre-registered `Unit M1` (`SFT -> ZPD-GRPO`) test.
