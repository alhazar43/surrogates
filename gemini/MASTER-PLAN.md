### 1.1 Admitting Failure Cleanly (`What Item Parameters CANNOT Tell Us`) & The Governing Prior-Scaling Law

Every recent paper applying Item Response Theory to Large Language Models (*Fluid Benchmarking*, *tinyBenchmarks*, *Position: IRT for AI*, *SEELE*, *RIDE*, *ZPD Detector*) rests on two unexamined assumptions borrowed from cross-sectional human testing:
1. **Molenaar's Ergodicity Fallacy (Between-Model $\neq$ Within-Model)**: Assuming that item discrimination $a_j^{\text{pop}}$ and difficulty $b_j^{\text{pop}}$ calibrated across a heterogeneous zoo of 102 models (spanning `0.1B` to `70B+` across pretraining corpora) remain structural coordinates along a *single* model's post-training trajectory (`SFT` or `RL`).
2. **Single-Moment Point-Mass Response ($\beta_s \equiv 1, \tau_s \equiv 1$)**: Assuming that a generative LLM in state $s$ responds to item $j$ as a 1-parameter Bernoulli point process moving along the external population curve $z_j^{\text{pop}} = a_j^{\text{pop}}(\theta_s - b_j^{\text{pop}})$.

**Why Plug-and-Play 2PL Replacement Failed (`Q1`, `Q2a`, `Q2-A`)**:
When we used static 102-LLM parameters $(a_j^{\text{pop}}, b_j^{\text{pop}})$ as a black-box 1D RL advantage multiplier (`Q1: pop0, pop1`) or 1D item-change/CAT replacement (`Q2-A, Q2a`), pre-registered rules failed (`Q1 Rule 4`: `0.4685–0.4748` band; `Q2-A Rule 1`: `0/6` `S1`, `1/12` `Q1`; `Q2c`: CAT loses by $n=100$). Why? Because post-training item log-odds $L_{s,j} = \operatorname{logit}(p_{s,j})$ obey the **Autoregressive Prior-Scaling Law** (`K1/consult.md §0.4, §1.2, §5.5`; `T9r`; `S1`; `Q2g`):

$$\boxed{L_{s,j} = \tau_s L_{\text{base},j} + \delta_s + \gamma_s z_j^{\text{pop}} + \varepsilon_{s,j} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \Delta L_{s,j} = L_{s,j} - L_{\text{base},j} = (\tau_s - 1) L_{\text{base},j} + \delta_s + \gamma_s z_j^{\text{pop}} + \varepsilon_{s,j}}$$

where $L_{\text{base},j}$ is the **base model's own initial prior log-odds**, $z_j^{\text{pop}} = a_j^{\text{pop}}(\theta_s - b_j^{\text{pop}})$ is the external population 2PL predictor, and by **Rule 4.1 (`project-rules.md`)**, the OLS regression slope $\hat{\tau}_s$ decomposes identically into **preference rank preservation $r_{s,\text{base}}$** and **scale dispersion ratio $\sigma_s / \sigma_{\text{base}}$**:
$$\hat{\tau}_s = \frac{\operatorname{Cov}_j(L_{s,j}, L_{\text{base},j})}{\operatorname{Var}_j(L_{\text{base},j})} = \underbrace{r(L_s, L_{\text{base}})}_{\text{Preference Correlation}} \cdot \underbrace{\frac{\sigma(L_s)}{\sigma(L_{\text{base}})}}_{\text{Scale Dispersion Ratio}}$$

### 1.2 `A helps B`: What Item Parameters CAN Tell Us — The Declarative vs. Procedural Neuro-Cognitive Double Dissociation ($R^2 = 76.9\%$)

If cross-sectional 2PL item parameters $(a_j^{\text{pop}}, b_j^{\text{pop}})$ are not within-model trajectory slopes, **what DO they measure, and how does psychometric & neuro-cognitive theory (`A`) explain LLM post-training (`B`)?**

In cognitive psychology and computational neuroscience (e.g., Squire, 1992; Anderson's ACT-R; Aston-Jones & Cohen, 2005 on neural gain modulation), learning and memory dissociate into two fundamental systems:
1. **Declarative / Semantic Memory (High $a_m^{\text{pop}}$, Low $b_m^{\text{pop}}$ in the 102-LLM Zoo)**: Factual/conceptual associations (*Marketing, Sociology, Prehistory, Geography, World History, Biology, Philosophy, Clinical Knowledge*). Across 102 LLMs of varying parameter count and pretraining scale, factual storage scales tightly and monotonically with model size—yielding **high population discrimination ($\bar{a}_m^{\text{pop}} = 0.69\text{–}1.45$) and low population difficulty ($\bar{b}_m^{\text{pop}} = -0.99\text{ to }+0.72$)**.
2. **Procedural / Multi-Step Quantitative Reasoning (Low $a_m^{\text{pop}}$, High $b_m^{\text{pop}}$ in the 102-LLM Zoo)**: Multi-step symbolic/mathematical computation (*High School Math, Physics, Statistics, Elementary Math, Econometrics, Macroeconomics*). Under standard leaderboard scoring across 102 diverse base/instruct models, multi-step math is noisy and poorly separated by raw pretraining scale alone—yielding **low population discrimination ($\bar{a}_m^{\text{pop}} = 0.28\text{–}0.55$) and high population difficulty ($\bar{b}_m^{\text{pop}} = +0.25\text{ to }+2.30$)**. ### 1.3 `B helps A`: What Counterfactual LLM Rollouts Teach Psychometric Theory (The Generative $(\theta_s, \beta_s)$ Model & 5 Mathematical Corollaries)

In human psychometrics, we never observe $K=32$ independent counterfactual realizations of the *exact same brain state* on the *exact same item* before and after controlled interventions on schema acquisition (`SFT`) vs. neural gain (`RL`). Our `1.48M` LLM rollouts provide this counterfactual laboratory, motivating the **Two-Parameter Generative Respondent Model**:

Let model state $s$ on item $j \in \{1, \dots, J\}$ generate $K$ rollouts $z_{sjk} \sim \pi_s(\cdot \mid x_j)$. Each rollout induces a latent decision margin $Z_{sjk}$:
$$Z_{sjk} = \mu_{sj} + \beta_s^{-1} \varepsilon_{sjk}, \qquad \varepsilon_{sjk} \stackrel{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim} \text{Logistic}(0, 1)$$
where $\mu_{sj}$ is the model's **latent item location** and $\beta_s > 0$ is its **within-item trajectory concentration / neural gain** (inverse rollout temperature; $\beta_s \to \infty \iff H_s \to 0$), so a single rollout succeeds with probability $p_{sj}(\theta_s, \beta_s) = \sigma(\beta_s \mu_{sj})$ and log-odds $L_{s,j} = \beta_s \mu_{sj} = \tau_s L_{\text{base},j} + \delta_s + \varepsilon_{s,j}$.

This single generative law mathematically derives all five empirical paradoxes across `S1`, `Q1`, and `Q2`:

---

#### **Corollary 1 / Proposition 1 (The Readout Crossover `Mean@16` vs. `Maj@16`/`Pass@16` in `S1`)**
Consider the three standard evaluation functionals over $J$ items as $K$ rollouts are drawn per item:
1. **Single-Draw Expectation (`Mean@K` / `Pass@1`)**: $\mathcal{M}_1(\theta_s, \beta_s) = \mathbb{E}_j\big[\sigma(\beta_s \mu_{sj})\big]$.
2. **Majority Vote (`Maj@K`)**: As $K \to \infty$, majority voting is **invariant to concentration $\beta_s$** and depends solely on the modal location boundary:
   $$\mathcal{M}_{\text{maj}}(\theta_s) = \lim_{K\to\infty} \text{Maj@}K(s) = \mathbb{P}_j(\mu_{sj} > 0)$$
3. **Best-of-$K$ Coverage (`Pass@K`)**: $\mathcal{M}_{\text{pass}, K}(\theta_s, \beta_s) = \mathbb{E}_j\!\left[1 - \big(1 - \sigma(\beta_s \mu_{sj})\big)^K\right]$.

**Derivation of the Crossover**:
Hold every item's latent location $\mu_{sj}$ (and thus $\mathcal{M}_{\text{maj}}$) fixed, and differentiate $\mathcal{M}_1$ with respect to concentration $\beta_s$:
$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{M}_{\text{maj}}}{\partial \beta_s} = 0, \qquad \frac{\partial \mathcal{M}_1}{\partial \beta_s} = \mathbb{E}_j\!\left[\mu_{sj} \, p_{sj}(1 - p_{sj})\right] = \operatorname{Cov}_j\!\big(\mu_{sj}, \, V_{\text{roll},sj}\big) + \mathbb{E}_j[\mu_{sj}]\mathbb{E}_j[V_{\text{roll},sj}]$$
where $V_{\text{roll},sj} = p_{sj}(1-p_{sj})$ is the within-item rollout variance.
- On 4-option multiple-choice benchmarks where wrong-mode items ($\mu_{sj} < 0$) sit near the lower boundary ($p_{sj} \approx 0.15 \implies V_{\text{roll}} \approx 0.12$) and right-mode items ($\mu_{sj} > 0$) sit in the interior ($p_{sj} \approx 0.55 \implies V_{\text{roll}} \approx 0.25$), we have $\mathbb{E}_j[\mu_{sj} p_{sj}(1-p_{sj})] > 0$.
- Consequently, GRPO ($\tau_{\text{GRPO}} > 1, \Delta\beta_s \gg 0$) **strictly increases `Mean@16`** (`0.479` vs. SFT `0.439`, `+4.0 to +6.1 SE`) while collapsing rollout entropy (`0.281` nats), yielding **almost zero Condorcet voting gain (`Maj@16 - Mean@16 = +0.002` unstarred / `+0.011*` tie-correct)** and collapsing `Pass@16` (`0.656`).
- Conversely, SFT ($\tau_{\text{SFT}} < 1$) preserves high rollout entropy (`0.943` nats) while shifting item locations on procedural domains (`raw mode change from Base over all 1,500 bank items: 0.560–0.564` for SFT vs. `0.398–0.436` for GRPO in `results/S1/rewrite1_mode_change.csv`; `r(L_SFT, L_base) = 0.733` vs. `0.858` GRPO in `S1`), generating a massive **`+0.065` (`+0.116*` tie-correct) Condorcet voting boost** that overtakes GRPO on `Maj@16` (`0.504` vs. `0.481`; `0.529` vs. `0.485` random tie-break; `0.555*` vs. `0.490*` tie-correct) and reaches `Pass@16 = 0.966` (`+31.0 SE` over GRPO).

#### **Corollary 3 / Proposition 3 (The Person-Fit Sign Flip Theorem $l_z^{(1)} = +2.24 \to l_z^{(32)} = -482$ & Why `Q2-A` Item Residuals Fail)**
1. **Single-Draw Hyper-Concentration ($k=1, l_z^{(1)} = +2.03\text{ to }+2.52$)**:

2. **Multi-Draw Binomial Overdispersion ($K=32, l_z^{(32)} \to -577, \text{SSR}_{\text{median}} \to 12.9$)**:
   Under a $\text{Binomial}(K, \hat{p}_{sj}^{\text{2PL}})$ model, the expected standardized squared residual decomposes as:
   $$\mathbb{E}[\text{SSR}_{sj}] = \frac{p_{sj}(1-p_{sj})}{\hat{p}_{sj}^{\text{2PL}}(1-\hat{p}_{sj}^{\text{2PL}})} + K \cdot \frac{\big(p_{sj} - \hat{p}_{sj}^{\text{2PL}}\big)^2}{\hat{p}_{sj}^{\text{2PL}}(1-\hat{p}_{sj}^{\text{2PL}})}$$
   Because all $K=32$ rollouts share the same model prior $L_{\text{base},j} \neq z_j^{\text{pop}}$, the second term multiplies curve divergence by $K=32$, driving $l_z^{(32)}$ to `-454` (`Q1 grpo`), `-482` (`S1 grpo`), and `-577` (`Q1 pop0`). *(Note: `lz` and `ssr_median` track $\beta_s$ monotonically across all 19 states, whereas mean `MSSR` is non-monotonic because a single bank outlier `prehistory_test_75` with $a_j = 8.67, b_j = -2.18, a_jb_j = -18.89$ contributes $\sim 1.5\times 10^5$ whenever $y_{sj}=0$.)*
3. **Why Both 1-Param and 2-Param Population Residuals ($c_{s,j}$) Fail `Q2-A` Item-Change Detection**:
   - In 1-parameter `q2_full.py` (`c_sj = e_sj - e_base,j`), $+a_j^{\text{pop}}b_j^{\text{pop}}$ cancels identically, so $(c_{sj} - \bar{c}_s) - k_{sj} = -(a_j^{\text{pop}} - \bar{a}^{\text{pop}})\Delta\hat{\theta}_s$ has variance `0.0158 logit²` (SFT) to `0.3616 logit²` (GRPO) against `~6.2 logit²` of $\Delta L_{sj}$, tying classical $|k_{sj}|$ (`0.654 vs. 0.645` in `S1`, `0/6` pairs; `0.625 vs. 0.603` in `Q1`, `1/12` pairs; `A2` false discovery share `0.31 > 0.15` limit).

When $(\tau_s - 1)L_{\text{base},j}$ is removed (`T9r`), cross-seed top-10% overlap *drops* (`0.726 -> 0.689` GRPO, `0.748 -> 0.422` SFT), proving that what looked like "item-specific learning" across seeds was actually global scaling of the shared Base prior $L_{\text{base},j}$.

#### **Corollary 4 / Proposition 4 (The $a_j^{\text{pop}}$-Weighting Attribution Reversal Theorem in `Q2-B`: `60% / 40%` Accuracy vs. `21% / 79%` 2PL $\hat{\theta}$)**
From the 2PL MLE score equation $\sum_{j=1}^J a_j^{\text{pop}}\big(p_{s,j} - \sigma(a_j^{\text{pop}}(\hat{\theta}_s - b_j^{\text{pop}}))\big) = 0$, a first-order Taylor expansion around a reference state gives:
$$\Delta \hat{\theta} \approx \frac{\sum_{j=1}^J a_j^{\text{pop}} \Delta p_j}{\sum_{j=1}^J (a_j^{\text{pop}})^2 p_j(1-p_j)} \propto \overline{\Delta p} + \frac{\operatorname{Cov}_j(a_j^{\text{pop}}, \, \Delta p_j)}{\bar{a}^{\text{pop}}}, \qquad \text{whereas} \qquad \Delta \text{Acc} = \overline{\Delta p} = \frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^J \Delta p_j$$
- In `Q2g/s1/precision.csv`, the bootstrap SE ratio $\text{SE}(\Delta\hat{\theta})/\text{SE}(\Delta\text{acc})$ is nearly constant across all contrasts (`6.73` Base–SFT, `7.68` SFT–GRPO, `7.98` Base–GRPO). Thus $\hat{\theta}$ does **not** reduce noise; it reweights the numerator by $\operatorname{Cov}_j(a_j^{\text{pop}}, \Delta p_j)$!
- By the **Declarative–Procedural Double Dissociation (Table 1.2)** and `Q2g/s1/flags.csv` (Reading A3):
